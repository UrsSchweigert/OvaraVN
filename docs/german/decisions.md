# German version: owner decisions

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-24. The glossary (`glossary.md`) holds the proposals; this file holds what the owner chose. Translators must follow it.

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hero's name | Keep "Wrack". "It's what we call what the tide brings in" becomes "Strandgut" or "Treibgut". The plant "bitterwrack" becomes "Bittertang". |
| 2 | Book title | "Der Mann, den die Flut brachte" |
| 3 | "hold" (Maker-built community) | "die Halte" (plural "die Halten"). The owner's own word. Check how it reads in the sample chapter: it can look like the verb form "halte". |

| 8 | Place names | Proper names stay (Low Anthem, Saltgate, Keelhaven, Port Halcyon). Descriptive names are translated: Girdle = Gürtel, Belt = Sturmgürtel, Wells = Brunnen, Lantern = Laterne, Hush = das Schweigen, Quiet = die Stille. "The Pale" and "Bright Shelves" are still open. |

| 10 | Chapter 18 and the city "Meet" | Keep "Meet" |

## Style decisions (owner, 2026-11-24)

All followed the glossary's recommended default. Items 4, 5, 6 and 11 of section H were already settled by decisions 8, 5, 3 and 7 above.

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| du / Sie | **Final rule (owner, 2026-11-24, second version; replaces the earlier ones):** (1) A Threaded person ("Fadenträger") says "du" to everyone. (2) A person who is not Threaded always says "Sie" to a Threaded person, whatever their ages. (3) Between two people who are both not Threaded: the younger says "Sie" to the older, the older says "du" to the younger, people of about the same age say "du", children are "du". (4) Always "du", both ways, in every chapter, whatever the other rules say: Sefa, Wrack and Ione with each other; and Okonta and Wrack with each other (owner addition, 2026-11-24). (4b) Exception (owner, 2026-11-24): Jef and Maelis say "du" to everyone, including Threaded people (Wrack, Okonta, Anselm Rook), in every chapter. (4c) Exception (owner, 2026-11-24): Hesper and Wrack/Ilan always say "du" to each other, both ways, in every chapter. (4d) Exception (owner, 2026-11-24): Sefa and Maelis always say "du" to each other. (5) Wrack counts as Threaded from the chapter 4 scene where he receives the Thread ("He woke in the box bed"); before that he is not Threaded. Interpretation to confirm: the earlier test "gifted / not gifted" is replaced by "Threaded / not Threaded". Per-pair tables in docs/german/address.md. |
| Tense and register | Präteritum, third person; plain spoken dialogue; no archaic words except the few named in the glossary (Pforte, Siel) |
| Dialect | None anywhere. Blunt characters (Dace, Brask) get short sentences, not slang. |
| Quotes and dashes | „…“ for speech, ‚…‘ for nested quotes. No en dashes and no em dashes: use a plain hyphen (-) if a break is needed, otherwise a comma, colon or full stop. Check the quote glyphs in the game font once on screen. (Owner correction, 2026-11-24.) |
| Italics | Keep every {i} span around the same words. No new italics, none removed. |
| Units | Metric (Meter, Kilometer, Tonne; Schritt, Handbreit). |
| Gender | The Dragon is der Drache / er; ships and boats are neuter (das Schiff, das Boot), the named boat is "die Geduld"; the sea is das Meer in narration and die See when a mariner speaks of it as a person. |
| Gender-neutral language | Rephrase singular "they" (man, jemand ... er oder sie). No gender stars or colons. Generic masculine plural for mixed groups; feminine for known all-female groups; individuals follow their sex (Ältester / Älteste, Heilerin). |
| Part titles | Translate them and the subtitle; add no part cards. |
| Names | Keep all names except Bother. |
| Chapter headings | "Kapitel 3. Der Drache"; parts "Teil Zwei: Der Alte". |
