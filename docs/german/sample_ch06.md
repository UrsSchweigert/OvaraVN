## Open: the chapter title

"The Eldest Brother" appears only as the title. The text never says who it means. I used the glossary proposal **Der älteste Bruder** and did not decide it.

## Choices to look at

- **Anselm Rook "I am he":** "Der bin ich." The English plays on "a Threaded who went up to the islands": the story the Threaded tell.
- **"paper":** Corin's "a paper in the speaker's hand" and Rook's "with paper" are both **Papier**. The glossary leaves "paper" open (about 24 uses).
- **"Silence":** Wrack knows the Slick by **die Lautlosigkeit**, as in chapter 2 (not "Stille", which is the south region, and not "Schweigen", the Hush). At the graveside I used the verb ("schwiegen"), which is a plain verb and not the Hush.
- **"intended water"** is **gehegtes Wasser** (Sefa stitches with it).
- **"They ask her to stand ready":** Okonta calls the sea **die See** and **sie**, a person (a mariner speaking), and the water "eine Haut aus Wasser".
- **"You can Hold":** **Das Halten beherrschen Sie.** The verb "to hold" (halten) and the form are one word, as the glossary says.
- **"Please-" in italics:** `*Bitte -*` with a plain hyphen, because en and em dashes are banned. "He could not have said *please*" is `*bitte*`.
- **Rook "I should like to see it run":** **Ich würde ihn gern laufen sehen** ("ihn" is the Dragon), repeated word for word by Wrack in box 199.
- **"The Lantern was struck... by a dart":** the dart (der Pfeil) hit **die Laterne**.
- **"Pilots. Guides.":** **Piloten. Lotsen.** (I avoided "Führer" for German reasons.)
- **"scrip":** **Firmengeld**.
- **"deeded ground":** **Land ... auf euch überschrieben**, not "Boden": it reads as ownership.
- **"the Tip":** **der Pol**.
- **"cake":** "ein Sack Kuchen" (kept; the English never says which cake).
- **"like a sack of cake" / "Any herder's child":** **Jedes Hirtenkind**.
- **"the heart-chamber":** **die Herzkammer** (the German word also means a chamber of a body heart; it is the chamber under the Lantern in chapter 5).
- **"Corin had the boy across his cradle":** I translated "cradle" as **Wiege**, as the English has it. In the book "cradle" is the berth in the Dragon; on a needle in the Slick it looks like a slip, perhaps "saddle". Possible slip in the source text: tell me and I will change it.
- **"He had us carry for him as well":** **Uns hat er auch für sich tragen lassen.**
- **"the Shoulder":** **auf der Schulter**.
