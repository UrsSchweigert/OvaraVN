# German glossary and style decisions (for the owner to approve before translation)

This list fixes one German rendering for every important name, place, thing and idea, so all 18 chapters and the game screens agree. Every row comes from the approved game text (`work/cut/ch01.md` to `ch18.md`), the world bible and the game files. Counts are rough, taken from the approved text (so a term cut from the story is not counted).

**How to answer:** in each row of the tables, mark the PROPOSED German as **Accept**, **Change to ...** or **Discuss**. Answer the numbered questions in section H the same way. The recommended default is given for each. Nothing is translated yet.

Reading the tables: "ch03, ~40x" means first seen in chapter 3, about 40 times in the whole book. A dash means there is no second option worth listing. "Keep" means the English form stays unchanged in German.

**The six hardest decisions are marked DECISION in the notes.** They are summed up in the report that came with this file.

## H. Style decisions for the owner

Each has a recommended default. Mark Accept or Change.

1. **Address: du or Sie between characters.**
   Recommended rule: *du* inside the mariner world (holds, healers and patients, Threaded among themselves, elders to the young and the young to elders), among Driftfolk and dockers, in families, and with children. *Sie* for agravars of rank or in formal settings: Rook and Venn with anyone; Marsh, Crane and the Meridian soldiers with Ilan; Lockkeepers to customers; officers; any first meeting between a mariner and a corporate person. Suggested special cases (my proposal, the text does not say): Ilan and Raske start with *Sie* (ch09-10) and move to *du* from ch12, when she brings her forty to the Moot (he already calls her "Teo" in ch09); Elder Vey uses *Sie* to everyone, as a mark of her cold formality. In the app text (menus, paywall): *du* matches the tone of the book, *Sie* is safer for an App Store listing: owner decides (default: *du*).

2. **Tense and register.** Narration in the *Präteritum*, third person, as in the original; dialogue in plain, spoken German without dialect. No archaic words except the few named in the table (Pforte, Hort, Siel). Default: yes.

3. **Dialect or speech quirks.** The text has none: no dialect, no stutter, no oaths (I found no swear words at all). Dace is blunt and Brask is blunt: reflect it with short sentences, not with slang. Default: no dialect anywhere.

4. **Place names.** Keep proper names of holds and cities; translate the descriptive regions (Girdle, Belt, Pale, Quiet, Hush, Well, Lantern, Crown, Bright Shelves, Sluice, Waterline). Default: as in group C. Risk: Deep Chorus, Nine Spires, Tidehall and Pearl Stair could also be translated.

5. **The two peoples.** Default: *Agravare* kept (the text glosses it as "land-dweller") and *Meerleute* for mariners. Alternative: keep "Mariner" as a pair of invented-sounding names, which is closer to the original but reads as navy ("Marine") to some German readers.

6. **Hold.** Default: *Hort* (plural *Horte*). This single word appears about 180 times in several senses; the alternatives are *Siedlung* (plain, but weak for "Hold Wars") and *Feste* (clash with "Feste" = feasts).

7. **Quotation marks and dashes.** The English text uses straight double quotes for speech and `--` or an em dash for breaks. Default: German „...“ for speech and ‚...‘ for nested quotes, with plain hyphens (-) for breaks if needed, never en or em dashes (owner decision, see decisions.md). Text bubbles in Ren'Py: the font must have these glyphs (check once).

8. **Italics `{i}`.** The text uses italic for the rule lines (*Ask. Do not order.*), the long-call phrases, signs and thoughts (about 108 spans). Default: keep every `{i}` span and place it around the same words; no new italics, none removed. Onomatopoeia is nearly absent; sounds are described in words, not written out.

9. **Units.** Keep metric: *Meter*, *Kilometer*, *Tonne*. "Paces" = *Schritt*; "a hand's width" = *eine Handbreit*; "forty metres high" = *vierzig Meter hoch*. Dates are not used. Default: yes.

10. **Gender of the Dragon, of ships, of the sea.** The Dragon is "it" in the text; in German it must be *der Drache* / *er* (a machine that people take for a creature). Boats: *das Schiff / das Boot* (neutral); the named boat is *die Geduld* (feminine). The sea: *das Meer* in narration; *die See* (feminine) when a mariner talks of the sea as a person ("she can die"). Default: as stated.

11. **The Moot.** Default: *das Moot*, plural *die Moots*, kept as a word the reader learns by use. Alternatives: *die Große Versammlung* (plain), *das Thing* (old Germanic assembly; risky because of its political history).

12. **The gender-neutral "they".** The text uses "they/them" for plural groups. Singular "they" appears about 3 times ("somebody ... they"). Default: rephrase with *man* or *jemand ... er oder sie*; no gender stars, no colons. Groups: use the generic masculine plural for mixed or unspecified groups and the feminine for known all-female groups (for example most healers shown). Individuals follow their sex (Ältester / Älteste, Heilerin).

13. **Epigraph and part pages.** There is no epigraph. The subtitle "A novel of Ovara" and the six part titles exist as headings only; the game has no part card in the scripts I checked, only the part name in the chapter menu. Default: translate them as in group F; no new pages.

14. **Names that mean something.** Default: keep all names except Bother. Wrack stays because German "Wrack" fits. Names like Rook, Crane, Marsh, Dace, Rime, Fallow are English words only for an English reader; the owner may prefer to translate Fallow or Rime. Default: do not.

15. **Chapter heading format.** Default: "Kapitel 3. Der Drache"; part pages: "Teil Zwei: Der Alte". Chapter numbers stay.

## I. Open questions I could not answer from the text

1. **"The Eldest Brother" (ch06 title).** The words appear only as the title. The chapter covers Corin and the forty-one Threaded; who the title points at is not stated. The proposed German assumes it means the same in the book as the title says.
2. **"Meet" (ch18).** The last chapter ends by naming the city "Meet", but the text does not say whether it is a verb ("to meet") or a noun. The proposed *Begegnung* fits both.
3. **Where the part titles are shown in the game.** The approved chapter texts carry only the chapter headings; I found the part names only in `chapter_select.rpy`. Whether title cards are planned is not decided.
4. **"Meridian's paper" and "the paper".** The word *paper* appears about 24 times in several senses (the purple flimsy, notes of debt held by Meridian, a newspaper-like sheet at Fallow). I did not give one German word for all.
5. **"Site Nine".** The place is named only in the chapter title; inside the story it is "the sill" and "the drowned city". A German title may therefore not match any word in the text.
6. **"touched" and "reach" as terms of rank.** The world bible grades callers (Touched, Caller, Mighty, Chorus, Unheard of). The approved text uses only "Mighty" and "chorus" as terms. I did not invent German grades beyond them.
7. **Does Ren'Py ship German stock strings?** I did not check. The 25 or so help lines (Enter, Space, Page Up ...) are stock text.
8. **Sex of a few minor characters** (Tam, Calla, Brin, Sabel). The text gives little; titles for them are avoided in the tables. The art canon calls Calla a woman only by implication, so I did not rely on it.
9. **Sefa's explanation of the name.** If "Wrack" is kept, her line "It's what we call what the tide brings in" needs a German noun for what the tide brings (Strandgut or Treibgut). That is a sentence-level choice for the translator, to be settled when ch01 is translated.
10. **Bible terms not in the approved text** (day-song, Charter Wars, Long Forgetting, Ease, Calling stones count, dragon almanacs plural). I left them out of the tables except where marked "not in the text".
