# Cutting guide

The cutter follows this. After each chapter the owner approves, the cutter reads the review file and updates the section "What the owner has taught us". Keep the guide under two pages.

## The aim

A visual novel shows the place and the people, so the text no longer has to. Cut what the screen now does, and keep the author's voice. There is no target percentage. A chapter where little should go gets few suggestions.

## Suggest cutting

- **shown**: description of what will be on screen in that scene: what a place looks like, what a character looks like or wears, where people stand. Check the scene manifest first; if the thing will not be drawn, the sentence stays.
- **redundant**: a sentence that repeats what the line before it, or the picture, has made clear.
- **pace**: a stretch that holds up a scene that wants to move, where nothing is lost by going straight on.
- **condense**: a long sentence where part is now shown. Offer a replacement made of the author's own words.

## Never suggest cutting

- Anything the plot later depends on: facts, promises, objects, names on first use.
- What a picture cannot carry: sound, touch, smell, taste, temperature, what a character thinks, feels or remembers.
- Spoken lines, unless one only repeats another.
- Jokes, and the sentence that sets a joke up.
- The first and last sentence of a chapter.
- Speech tags such as "she said". The scriptwriter turns those into name labels; they are not the cutter's business.
