# Style review, Kapitel 16 (Der lange Umweg)

## Overall note

The chapter is faithful and mostly idiomatic. The Lockkeeper, Elder Vey and Maelis each sound right, and the fixed lines (the rule of the Threaded, Maelis's question) match the earlier chapters word for word. Du/Sie is correct in every pair.
Weakest: three places where a pronoun or case changes the meaning (paras 25, 44, 84), and a group of loose participles ("stehen bleibend", "weiterreichend", "schleifend") that the English "-ing" forms pulled in.
Patterns to watch: "es" with no neuter noun behind it (paras 15, 88), "Das habe ich." for "I did.", "nie ein einziges Mal", and the sea-wall being "Ufermauer" here but "Hafenmauer" in chapter 14.
Paragraph numbers count from the first paragraph after the heading, scene breaks not counted (98 paragraphs).

## Questions for the owner

1. "Canyon" is "die Schlucht" in paras 47 and 55. The glossary has "Kanyon". Chapter 5 uses both words side by side, so I did not list it. Do you want "Kanyon" only?
2. Para 8: "I'm nobody's pilot" is "Ich bin niemandes Pilot". The glossary gives "Lotse" for Meridian's "pilot"; chapter 6 has "Piloten. Lotsen." together. Which word is Corin's?
3. Para 23: "To whom?" is "Zu wem?". After "Komm, wenn du gerufen wirst" this reads as "come to whom?". If Okonta means "to whom do you swear it?", it should be "Wem?" and the answer "Dir. Du lebst."
4. Para 88: "I did one of those" is "Eines von denen habe ich gemacht". The German is as unclear as the English. If she means a dead place like Fallow, "So eine Stelle habe ich auch gemacht" would say it.
5. Para 82: "I should like him to see mine" is "dass er meines sieht" (her game). If "mine" means her memory on the city's walls, the neuter "meines" would be wrong.
