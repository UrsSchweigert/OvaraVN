# Style review, chapter 11 (Taubes Wasser)

Not approved. Suggestions only; the owner decides. Nothing in `ch11.md`, `game/` or `source/` was changed.

## Overall note

The hardest chapter of the six, and the German carries it. The short sentences of the order and its aftermath (paras 38 to 58) are as bare as the original, and the night scene with Sefa is right in tone.
Weakest: two real meaning errors in plain-looking sentences ("where they were going", "saw them over the ridge"), and the verb "herausgeben" for "put out of himself", which reads as "hand over" and blurs the image of the emptied room.
Patterns to watch: (1) lines that echo ch10 word for word in English but not in German (the nail, "The engine isn't sorry"); (2) Okonta's sentence about the rule, worded three ways in ch06, ch07 and ch11; (3) "Lastkahn" (the waste barge) used for the Lashing barge.
The new sentences fit. "dunkel verbrannt" (Hesper, also in ch12) suggests burns rather than weathering.

## Questions for the owner

1. **"*Bitte*, Wrack." (para 28).** Okonta's "*Ask*, Wrack" is the imperative of "bitten", by decision 6. In this spot a German reader will first hear "please". It is the decided form, so I leave it; "*Bitte darum*, Wrack" would remove the doubt if you want.
2. **"Es" for what takes Sefa's gift (paras 57, 76, 91).** The original never names it. German "es" works, but in para 91 ("Es hat Sefas Gabe genommen") a reader may look for a neuter noun. Ione says "das Meer" in ch12. Keep "es"?
3. **"Sie ist fort" (para 80).** The old caller says "She's gone" of the sea, so "sie" = die See, per the gender decision. No noun is near. Fine as decided; only noting it.
4. **"das tote Wasser" (para 108).** The original says "dead water" here, in the chapter called "Deaf Water". The glossary keeps the two apart, and the German follows the original. No change suggested.
