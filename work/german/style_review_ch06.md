# Style review, Kapitel 6

## Overall note

A good chapter: Rook's speech has the right cool courtesy, the ambush is fast, and Okonta's lesson is clear. All Threaded say "du", as decided, and Wrack now does too.
Errors that matter: "woran er sich ... schämte" is not a German construction (para 55); "abgefeuert" for the dart that was "driven" (steered) from a hull (para 40); "anlehnen" for "lean", which is "sich neigen" everywhere else and is the point of the comparison with the needle (para 83).
Consistency with earlier chapters is the main thing to watch here: "Teiche" for the Lantern's pools (ch04: "Becken"), "Krabben" for Jef's crabs (ch04: "Krebse"), "Piloten. Lotsen." although "pilot" is "Lotse" in the glossary, "Maschinen" for engines (ch03: "Motoren"), "ganz oben auf der Welt" (elsewhere "am oberen Ende der Welt"). Para 96 quotes two of Jef's lines from ch04 in a slightly different (and better) wording; I have listed the matching changes in the ch04 review.
Calques are fewer than before ("mein Wort gegen ihres zu nehmen", "als es zu Schlägen kam", "Ende für Ende", "neunhundert Jahre davon"). The nine new sentences fit.

## Questions for the owner

1. "ground" in Rook's offer: ch04 para 124 has "Boden" ("Ich kann ihnen Boden geben. Echten Boden"), ch06 para 33 has "Land" ("Land. Ein Stück davon, auf euch überschrieben"), and the glossary additions give "Land" for ch15 to ch18. It is the central thing Rook sells, so one word would be stronger. Which?
2. Chapter title "Der älteste Bruder" (open in decisions.md): after reading the chapter closely I think it points at Rook, who has carried the Thread longest ("länger als jeder von euch") and so is the eldest of the brotherhood. The German title fits that reading. No change needed; only my view, since the point is open.
3. "Er hätte auf einen Aufzug warten können" (para 24): "lift" could also mean a ride. "Aufzug" is my reading too, but the mariner world has no lifts, so the image is from Wrack's forgotten life. Fine if meant.
4. "wie einen Sack Kuchen" (para 81): the English "a sack of cake" means kelp cake (trade goods). "wie einen Sack Kelpkuchen" would be clearer; "Kuchen" alone sounds like pastry.
