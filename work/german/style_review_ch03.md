# Style review, Kapitel 3

## Overall note

The chapter carries the long journey well; Okonta's dry voice and Brask's bluntness both come through in short German sentences, and the Dragon's arrival keeps its build-up. Terms and forms of address follow the decisions (Brask and the Fallow woman say "Sie", Okonta "du").
Meaning errors are few but real: "aufgefischt" for the ship that was lifted (para 20), "weitertragen" for "carry us on [board]" (para 57), "Es läuft den ganzen Langruf entlang" (para 60), and the ambiguous "singt er" in para 55, which now says the Dragon sings, not the stone. One grammar slip: "Einen Kilometer ... liefen" (para 75).
Recurring calques: "oder so" for "or so" (paras 4, 53), "Eine einzige von mir", "Sie sind nicht viel", "Jeder hat das", "Nicht für irgendwen", and "seit vor" (para 56, like "seit bevor" in ch01).
"Halt den Mund" (para 38) is an unlucky idiom: in German it means "shut up". The new passages fit; no join problems found.

## Questions for the owner

1. "Herr?" for the officer's "Sir?" (para 70, glossary choice): a bare "Herr?" is not how a German officer would address a superior, and it may read oddly at such an important moment. I only note it; the glossary lists "Sir?" as the alternative.
2. "Wie ein Körperteil" for "Like a limb" (paras 3 and 4): correct but a little clinical. "Wie einen Arm" would be more concrete, but it narrows "limb". Keep?
3. "Wir füttern sie umsonst" (para 102): "füttern" is what one does with animals and small children. If Brask's rough joke is meant, keep it; if not, "wir ernähren sie umsonst".
4. Brask says "Sie" to Wrack in this chapter (rule 3, Wrack is older and not yet Threaded). From chapter 4 on the rules change for Wrack; please check that Brask's later lines follow the table in address.md.
