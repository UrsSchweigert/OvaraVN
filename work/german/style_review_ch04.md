# Style review, Kapitel 4

## Overall note

The longest chapter (230 paragraphs, about twice the others, hence a few more rows than 40) and the best sustained one: Jef's grumbling, Maelis's exact answers and Rook's calm all have their own German voice, and the death scene is as plain as the original. Forms of address follow the rules (Wrack says "Sie" to Jef, Maelis and Rook until he has the Thread).
Clear mistakes: "sehr heller, sehr dunkler Augen" and "hellen dunklen Augen" (paras 18, 152: "bright" eyes are lively, not light-coloured, so the German contradicts itself), four missing commas after a closing quotation mark (paras 64, 80, 133, 147, all in newly added speech tags), and "Schiffbruch" against the decision on wrecks (para 146).
Recurring patterns: "sich drehen" for the rays' circling (paras 25, 92, 107, 144); "tiefe Sonne" for a low sun (paras 8, 79); English auxiliaries copied ("hat das", "würde es nicht wissen", "ich konnte sagen"); and a few places where a pronoun or word order blurs who or what is meant (paras 53, 62, 156, 189, 193, 221).
The newly translated descriptions are good. Cross-chapter consistency: cod is "Dorsch" in ch03 and "Kabeljau" here; seal is "Robbe" in ch03 and "Seehund" here.

## Questions for the owner

1. Maelis's "Sorry" (para 103), decided as "Tut mir leid": I must flag this as a meaning problem, not as a suggestion. In the English, "Sorry" is her answer to "What was he like at twenty?": he was sorry (full of remorse, for Sounding), and she then says it in full: "He was very, very sorry." "Tut mir leid" turns the first word into an apology by Maelis, and the link between the two sentences is lost. A wording that keeps the short first word and the echo: „Reue“, sagte sie. „Er war voller Reue.“ Or: „Es tat ihm leid“, sagte sie. „Es tat ihm sehr, sehr leid.“
2. "Hallo, alter Mann" (para 113): Jef is "der Alte" everywhere else. Rook's greeting could be "Hallo, Alter" to match, but that sounds like youth slang today. I would keep "alter Mann"; please confirm.
3. "Tintenfische" for the well-wardens (para 59, glossary additions): these are giant squid with an eye like a cartwheel. "Tintenfisch" suggests something small on a plate; "Kalmare" is the exact word. Keep?
4. "Trage" (para 6) against "Bahre" for "litter" in ch05 (glossary additions): one word for both chapters?
5. "Narrenarbeit" for "a fool's job" (para 69): does the owner like the coinage? "Eine Arbeit für Narren" would be plainer.
