# Style review, chapter 7 (Die Tür)

Not approved. Suggestions only; the owner decides. Nothing in `ch07.md`, `game/` or `source/` was changed.

## Overall note

The chapter reads well. The voice is plain and restrained, and the long memory section (paras 73 to 92) keeps the short, flat sentences of the original. Strongest: the Hush scenes and the dialogue with Maelis. Weakest: a handful of word-for-word renderings that mean something else in German ("Sie ließ ihn sitzen", "Sie alle.", "auswendig", "ein paar Fremde").
Patterns to watch: (1) pronouns whose German gender points at the wrong noun ("ihre zentrale Halle", "in seinem Inneren"); (2) lines that are quoted again in other chapters but worded differently here (Jef's "it doesn't get lighter", Okonta's "I said the rule to him"); (3) English word order kept ("Er hatte nicht gewusst, bis jetzt, ...").
The eight newly translated passages fit the surrounding text. Only two small points there (paras 16 and 29).

## Questions for the owner

1. **"Ältester Oro" after a preposition.** Para 69 has "Er dachte an Ältester Oro" (also ch01 "zu Ältester Oro", ch09 "von Ältester Oro"). Ch12 declines it with the article: "mit der Ältesten Vey". Decision 12 fixes the title before the name but not the case. Do you want "an den Ältesten Oro" / "vom Ältesten Oro" everywhere, or the undeclined title everywhere?
2. **"Lautlosigkeit" in para 28 and 57 to 58.** The decided word for the silence of dead water is used here for the listening silence of the Smokers and for the Hush ("die Lautlosigkeit eines Zimmers, in dem jemand lauscht"; "Sie führte ihn durch die Lautlosigkeit"). "Stille" would be the natural word in these three places. Keep as decided?
3. **Sefa's "Es ist so *offen*" (para 20) and "Okonta: griesgrämig" (para 21).** "sour" as the north's reputation is rendered "griesgrämig"; it loses the pair soft/sour ("weich" / "sauer"). Only a taste point, so not in the table.
