# Style review, chapter 10 (Der Tisch)

Not approved. Suggestions only; the owner decides. Nothing in `ch10.md`, `game/` or `source/` was changed.

## Overall note

The dinner scene is the best sustained dialogue in my six chapters. Rook's German is calm, exact and a little formal, which is right, and the du between Rook and Ilan gives the scene its closeness. Sefa's last speech also lands.
Weakest: three places where a short English line was misread ("It was very hard to see", "Ask me what you came to ask", "You said."), and several prepositions that are slightly off ("Auf zwanzig Jahre Dienst", "voll vom Schlick", "seit vor").
Patterns to watch: (1) very short English sentences, where one wrong word changes the sense; (2) recalled lines that must match their first wording (Jef in ch04, "untersten Gewölbe", "kantig"); (3) "instandsetzen" as one word in para 77.
The newly translated passages fit; only the triple "worden" in para 13 is heavy.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Para 87 to 90: "Es" or "Er" for the heart and the Dragon.** Ilan and Okonta speak about the Dragon without naming it ("There's only one that size ... It runs ... It never comes up"). German has to choose: "es" (das Herz) or "er" (der Drache). The text uses "es" and then "ihn" in the quoted line. It works, but if you want the reader to catch the Dragon sooner, "Er kommt nie herauf" in para 90 would do it.
2. **"Die Hirtin" (para 77).** Rook's word for Okonta ("The herder?"). Listed in the glossary additions; I only note that a reader may not connect it with Okonta, as in English.
3. **"Hauptdeck / Achterdeck / Kajüte" (para 13).** Chosen by the translator, all fine and standard.
