Anime-style wide illustration, clean line art, soft cel shading, gentle natural colours, cinematic composition, restrained and quiet, no text, no letters. Reference 1 is Maelis, a tall straight woman in her seventies with grey hair in a low bun and a patched dark charcoal coat. Reference 2 is Jef, a very old, slight, nearly bald man with white tufts over the ears, in a brown leather apron over a worn wool jersey, eyes closed. Reference 3 is the landing at dusk: red twilight at the top of the world, black still water, snow and ice. Exactly two people. Maelis stands on the dark stone landing and carries Jef lying across both of her arms, horizontally, as one adult carries another: her forearms under his back and under his knees, his head resting back against her shoulder. Jef has fully ADULT body proportions: his head is only about one seventh of his body height, his torso and long legs are those of a grown man, his whole body is as long as her arm span, his boots hang down past her hip. He is slight and thin but clearly a grown old man, NOT a child, NOT a doll, NOT small. His eyes are closed, his face peaceful and still, his arms and legs resting loosely, nothing clinging. Maelis wears only her own patched charcoal coat, no apron. Her face is composed, grief held quietly, looking out over the water. Beside the landing the huge pale ribbed machine, the Dragon, lies intact and calm on the black water, long tall pale ribs along a straight spine, unharmed, and along its whole length a faint cold BLUE light glows softly from within it, a pale blue glow shining through the ribs and reflecting on the water. Over the water beside it huge dark stingrays rise and fly through the air, wing beside wing, like an escort of sails. No other people.
