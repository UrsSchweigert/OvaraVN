Anime-style wide cinematic illustration, clean line art, soft cel shading, gentle natural colours, dynamic action, no text, no letters. Reference 1 is the colossal sheer wall of smooth grey glassy standing sea water, to keep: no seams, no panels, no rock, foam only along its top edge, mist at the foot, tiny barges far below on a grey sea. Reference 2 shows Okonta: a spare woman of about sixty, dark brown skin, short grey hair, close slate-grey high-collared suit; copy her face and look. Reference 3 shows Ilan: a lean man, short messy black hair, light skin, loose grey-green tunic, bare feet; copy his look.
Draw exactly three slender pale silver flying needles (each pointed at both ends, no window, no rudder, a soft light along the flank), small against the huge wall, climbing the face of the wall at different heights and different angles, close to the surface, in three sweeping diagonal arcs that fan out across the wall, at speed. The lowest needle (Okonta's), left and low, climbs steeply; the middle needle, right and higher, curves; the top needle is nearly at the foam top edge. Spray and mist streaks trail off the water face behind each needle. Riders lean forward lying along the needle, hair and clothes blown back by the wind.
Needle one: Okonta, a WOMAN with a lined face, no beard, dark brown skin and short grey hair (as in reference 2), lies in front steering it by leaning, determined face, slate-grey suit with high collar; behind her an engineer in a bulky grey hard suit with a round clear glass helmet clings to her back.
Needle two: Ilan rides ALONE, lying flat, bare feet, grey-green tunic blown by the wind, calm focused face; nobody behind him.
Needle three: a heavy man with a short grey beard and curly dark-grey hair, in a slate-grey suit, leads, and behind him an engineer in a bulky grey hard suit with a round clear glass helmet clings on, eyes shut.
Adult proportions, simple faces with fully drawn eyes, riders about the size of a thumb against the wall to show the dizzying sixty-metre height. Nothing else in the air.
